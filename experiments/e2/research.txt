================================================================================
e2 — LITERATURE SURVEY: TRAINING-FREE SHADOW REMOVAL
Target dataset: ISTD+ (Adjusted ISTD), 1330 train / 540 test
Compiled: 2026-10-25
================================================================================

PURPOSE
  Decide what a training-free shadow remover should actually DO, given that the
  project's own measurements (baseline/RESULTS.md) showed that:
    - the spread between five different training-free estimators on ISTD+ is
      only ~0.5 dB PSNR-S, so the estimator is not where the headroom is;
    - a per-pixel oracle gain field reaches 44 dB, but the guideline's Stage 5
      parameterisation throws away ~22 dB of it (smoothing -18, clamp -3.4,
      penumbra feather -14).
  So: the modelling assumptions and the penumbra, not a cleverer matcher, are
  where the remaining dB live. This survey was run to find out what the
  literature already knows about exactly those two things.

VERIFICATION LEGEND
  [V]  claim verified against the source during this session
  [K]  standard, widely-reported result stated from background knowledge;
       NOT re-verified here — check before citing in a paper
  [?]  found but not confirmed; treat as a lead only

================================================================================
PART 1 — THE SHADOW FORMATION MODEL  (what e2 is built on)
================================================================================

[1] Shor, Y. and Lischinski, D.
    "The Shadow Meets the Mask: Pyramid-Based Shadow Removal."
    Computer Graphics Forum (Eurographics) 27(2), 577-586, 2008.
    https://www.cs.huji.ac.il/~danix/ShadowRemoval/shadow_eg08.pdf
    [V] Lit intensity splits into a DIRECT and an AMBIENT term. A shadow
        removes the direct component and attenuates part of the ambient one.
    [V] This makes the relation between shadowed and lit intensity at a pixel
        AFFINE, with four unknown parameters, estimated from TWO STRIPS of
        pixels — one inside the shadow, one in the lit area.
    [V] Handles varying shadow intensity by processing from the shadow INTERIOR
        OUTWARD TOWARD THE BOUNDARY, and inpaints a thin border strip to blend.
    [V] The single-attenuation-factor assumption implies ambient light has the
        same colour in every direction — i.e. it is an approximation that fails
        under coloured or directional ambient light.
    >> THE KEY RESULT FOR e2. Every method in baseline/ uses a purely
       MULTIPLICATIVE gain, which is the affine model with the offset forced to
       zero. Forcing b=0 is wrong exactly where ambient light matters, which is
       the whole umbra.

[2] Le, H. and Samaras, D.
    "Shadow Removal via Shadow Image Decomposition." ICCV 2019.
    https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08628
    and the journal version:
    "Physics-based Shadow Image Decomposition for Shadow Removal." TPAMI 2021.
    https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13018
    [V] "A linear illumination transformation to model the shadow effects...
        allows the shadow image to be expressed as a combination of the
        shadow-free image, THE SHADOW PARAMETERS, and A MATTE LAYER."
    [V] SP-Net predicts the (per-image, per-channel) shadow parameters; M-Net
        predicts the shadow matte; I-Net inpaints to refine.
    [V] ISTD shadow-region RMSE 13.3 (prior SOTA) -> 7.9, and 7.4 when trained
        with their synthetic augmentation.
    [V] THIS IS THE ISTD+ DATASET PAPER — the adjusted shadow-free images this
        whole project uses come from here.
    >> Confirms the same affine form as [1], and adds the matte as the right
       way to handle the penumbra. Note that SP-Net LEARNS the parameters that
       [1] estimates from two strips: a training-free method can use [1]'s
       estimator inside [2]'s compositing equation.

[3] Finlayson, G., Hordley, S., Lu, C. and Drew, M.
    "On the Removal of Shadows from Images." TPAMI 28(1), 2006.
    (and "Entropy Minimization for Shadow Removal," IJCV 2009)
    [K] Illumination-invariant 1-D/2-D chromaticity image obtained by projecting
        log-chromaticity along the lighting-change direction; shadow edges are
        those present in the original gradient but absent in the invariant;
        zero them and re-integrate by solving a Poisson equation.
    [K] Fully training-free; needs no mask; but loses the absolute illumination
        level and is sensitive to camera spectral sharpening.
    >> Implemented in this project as baseline `gradient_domain`.
       MEASURED HERE: PSNR-S 21.11, i.e. the WORST of the five training-free
       baselines on ISTD+ except global_gain is better. Re-integration spreads
       boundary error over the whole region.

[4] Guo, R., Dai, Q. and Hoiem, D.
    "Paired Regions for Shadow Detection and Removal." TPAMI 35(12), 2013.
    (CVPR 2011 earlier version)
    [K] Region pairing: over-segment, classify regions lit/shadow, pair each
        shadow region with a lit region of the same material using texture and
        colour-ratio features, relight by the per-channel ratio of the pair.
    [V] Reported on ISTD+ in a recent comparison table: PSNR 25.52, SSIM 0.925,
        MAE 6.09 — the strongest classical number found in this survey.
    >> Implemented in this project as baseline `guo_paired_regions`.
       MEASURED HERE: PSNR-S 21.76, whole-image PSNR 28.88, "RMSE" 1.49 (best
       Lab error of any training-free baseline) but 1589 ms/image — 8x slower
       than the boundary-ring method that beats it on PSNR.

[5] Yang, Q., Tan, K. and Ahuja, N.
    "Shadow Removal Using Bilateral Filtering." TIP 21(10), 2012.
    [V] Reported on ISTD+ in the same comparison table as [4]: PSNR 20.26,
        SSIM 0.706, MAE 15.90 — i.e. BARELY ABOVE DOING NOTHING.
    >> Useful calibration: intrinsic-image/bilateral approaches that do not use
       a mask essentially fail on ISTD+.

[6] Gong, H. and Cosker, D.
    "Interactive Shadow Removal and Ground Truth for Variable Scene
    Categories." BMVC 2014.
    https://bmva-archive.org.uk/bmvc/2014/papers/paper004/index.html
    and "Interactive Removal and Ground Truth for Difficult Shadow Scenes,"
    JOSA A 33(9), 2016.
    [V] Models the shadow image as a per-channel HADAMARD PRODUCT of a shadow
        SCALE LAYER and the shadow-free image; lit-area scale = 1, shadowed
        scales in (0,1).
    [V] Needs only TWO ROUGH STROKES — one on the shadow, one on the lit area —
        and learns on the fly from them.
    [V] PENUMBRA: "registering the penumbra to a normalized frame, which allows
        us efficient estimation of nonuniform shadow illumination changes."
    >> THE SECOND KEY RESULT FOR e2. The penumbra profile is MEASURED from the
       image after normalising the boundary coordinate — not assumed to be a
       fixed ramp. Our own oracle decomposition says a fixed feather costs
       ~14 dB, so this is the single largest fixable loss.

[7] Xiao, C., Xiao, D., Zhang, L. and Chen, L.
    "Efficient Shadow Removal Using Subregion Matching Illumination Transfer."
    Computer Graphics Forum 32(7), 2013.
    (also "Shadow Remover: Image Shadow Removal Based on Illumination
    Recovering Optimization," TIP 2015)
    [?] Found via search; subregion matching + illumination transfer, close in
        spirit to [4] and to the project's guideline. Not verified in detail.

PART 3 — TRAINING-FREE / ZERO-SHOT DEEP METHODS
================================================================================

[11] "FreeShadow: Training-Free Shadow Removal via Illumination Transfer and
     Selective Content Preservation in Diffusion Models."
     https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26715
     [V] Training-free in the sense of using a FROZEN pretrained diffusion
         model (SD 2.1); no task-specific training, no test-time optimisation.
     [V] Its own comparison table is the source of the Guo [4] and Yang [5]
         ISTD+ numbers quoted above.
     >> "Training-free" here means "no shadow-specific training", not "no
        learned weights". It inherits an 800M-parameter prior and diffusion
        sampling latency. A different point on the axis from this project,
        and worth naming explicitly so the claim in guideline.md section 1.2
        stays precise.

[12] "Self-ShadowGAN" — zero-shot, learns from the single test image using a
     histogram-based discriminator.
     [V] Needs adversarial optimisation AT TEST TIME, so it is zero-shot but
         very slow, and [11] reports it produces artefacts.
     >> Relevant as the thing a fast analytic method should beat on latency by
        three or four orders of magnitude.

[13] "Deshadow-Anything: When Segment Anything Model Meets Zero-shot Shadow
     Removal." https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.11715  [?] not examined in detail.

[14] "FASR-Net: Unsupervised Shadow Removal Leveraging Inherent Frequency
     Priors." https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05779  [?] frequency-domain priors,
     unsupervised (still trained). Relevant to guideline.md's amplitude idea.

================================================================================
PART 4 — WHAT THIS SURVEY CHANGES ABOUT THE PLAN
================================================================================

FINDING 1 — THE MODEL IS WRONG, NOT JUST THE ESTIMATOR.
  [1] and [2] agree the shadow relation is AFFINE (gain + offset) because
  ambient light survives inside the umbra. Everything in baseline/ and in e1
  uses a purely multiplicative gain, i.e. offset forced to zero. That is a
  structural error, and it explains why five quite different multiplicative
  estimators all land within 0.5 dB of each other: they are all fitting the
  wrong family.
  => e2 ACTION: per-instance, per-channel AFFINE fit  I_free = a*I + b.

FINDING 2 — THE PENUMBRA SHOULD BE MEASURED, NOT ASSUMED.
  [6] normalises the penumbra coordinate and estimates the illumination change
  across it. [2] gives the compositing rule (a matte layer). Our oracle says a
  fixed feather costs ~14 dB.
  => e2 ACTION: build the matte from the image's OWN radial intensity profile
     across the mask boundary, per instance. No assumed ramp shape or width.

FINDING 3 — TWO STRIPS ARE ENOUGH, AND CHEAP.
  [1] estimates its four parameters from two strips; [6] from two strokes.
  Our `boundary_gain` baseline already showed thin rings beat whole-region
  statistics (22.04 vs 21.55 dB) at 197 ms.
  => e2 ACTION: keep the two-strip estimator, upgrade it from 1 parameter
     (ratio of medians) to 2 per channel (robust affine via matched quantiles),
     and keep the cost in the tens of milliseconds.

FINDING 4 — DO NOT CHASE PATCH MATCHING ON ISTD+.
  [4] is the best classical method in the literature and our tuned
  reimplementation of it is 8x slower than the boundary-ring method and loses
  to it on PSNR. ISTD+ is mostly single shadows on near-uniform ground, so
  there is little material diversity for region matching to exploit.
  => e2 ACTION: no segmentation, no patch matching, no descriptor search.
     This is also what keeps latency low.

FINDING 5 — "TRAINING-FREE" NEEDS DEFINING.
  [11] calls itself training-free while using a frozen 800M diffusion prior.
  => e2 ACTION: state the claim precisely — e2 has ZERO learned weights of any
     kind, and its only fitted quantities are a handful of scalars selected on
     the ISTD+ train split, each of which is a hyper-parameter and not a model.
